Same room · Same WiFi · No install

Two phones.
One game.

Fun Hub is a collection of five free two-player games that run in a mobile browser. Two people each use their own phone in the same room, paired by scanning a QR code. There is no app to install, no account to create and no internet connection needed beyond the local WiFi.

  1. Open the gameOne phone opens a game and shows a QR code.
  2. Scan to joinThe other phone scans it. Same WiFi, no app, no account.
  3. PlayThe phones talk directly to each other. One thumb each.

The games

Five games, all free, all in the browser.

  • Air Hockey

    Drag your thumb across your own half to slide your mallet and knock the puck into the goal slot behind your opponent. Each phone shows the table from its own end, and seven goals takes the match.

    • 2 players
    • 3 min
    • realtime

  • Chess

    A full game of chess played across two phones, each showing the board from its own side. Tap a man then tap its square; castling, en passant, promotion and threefold repetition are all enforced, with five minutes each and three seconds a move. Practice alone against a computer set to easy, normal or hard.

    • 2 players
    • 10 min
    • turns

  • Dawn Patrol

    Drag anywhere to bank your fighter and hold the FIRE button until the guns overheat. Parachuted crates multiply your rate of fire, damage and gun count for a few seconds, while mines and flak bursts hurt whoever is nearest; five kills takes the patrol.

    • 2 players
    • 3 min
    • realtime

  • Moonbloom

    Lead your moth with a fingertip and hold it over a closed bud until the bud opens in your colour. Both phones can work the same bud and nothing you have grown can be taken back; eight blossoms wins the night.

    • 2 players
    • 2 min
    • realtime

  • Orchard

    Swipe to steer your snake around a wrapping orchard and hold to sprint. Fruit makes you longer, bombs cost a hundred, and driving your nose into the other snake scores; five hits wins the match.

    • 2 players
    • 3 min
    • realtime

How two phones connect

Fun Hub pairs two phones by having one phone display a QR code and the other scan it, after which the phones exchange game data directly over the local WiFi using a WebRTC data channel. No game server sits in the middle and nothing about the match is stored.

Why a QR code instead of a room code

A typed room code is four to six characters of friction and a source of mistakes when two people are already holding their phones up. A QR code carries the full join link, including the host's local address and the session identifier, so the second player goes from camera to game in a single tap. If a camera is unavailable, every game also prints the join link underneath the code so it can be typed or shared by message.

Why WebRTC and not a WebSocket server

Real-time games are unplayable when every input has to travel to a data centre and back. A puck struck on one phone has to appear on the other in well under a tenth of a second, and a round trip to a hosted server usually costs more than that on its own. A WebRTC data channel between two phones on the same router typically settles in single-digit milliseconds. It also means running Fun Hub costs nothing per player: the pages are static files, and the traffic that matters never touches a server.

What the same-WiFi requirement really means

Both phones need to be on the same local network so they can reach each other directly. Home and office WiFi work. Phone hotspots work, as long as both devices are on the hotspot. Two problems come up in practice: guest networks and public WiFi in cafés and hotels often enable client isolation, which blocks devices from seeing each other, and one player quietly sitting on mobile data instead of WiFi will never pair. If a scan hangs, checking both phones are on the same network name solves it most of the time.

Privacy and what is stored

Nothing. There are no accounts, no cookies for tracking, no analytics on gameplay and no server-side record of a match. Scores live in the two browsers for the duration of the game and are gone when the tab closes. This is a side effect of the architecture rather than a policy: with no server in the loop, there is nowhere for data to accumulate.

Designing for one thumb

Every game is built around a single thumb on a phone held one-handed. Controls are drags, holds and taps within reach of that thumb, never multi-finger gestures or on-screen buttons at the top of the screen. Each phone also renders the shared world from its own player's point of view, so in Air Hockey and Chess both players see their own side at the bottom, exactly as they would sitting across a real table.

Questions

Do I need to install an app?

No. Every game and the hub itself run in a normal mobile browser. There is nothing to install, no account to create and no email address to hand over.

How do the two phones connect?

One phone hosts the game and shows a QR code; the other phone scans it. The two phones then open a direct WebRTC data channel to each other over your local WiFi, so game data does not pass through a server.

Do both phones need to be on the same WiFi?

Yes. The connection is made directly between the two phones on the local network, which is what keeps the round trip fast enough to play in real time. A phone on mobile data will usually fail to pair.

Can more than two people play?

No. Every game on Fun Hub pairs exactly two phones. Players can swap between rounds, but only two devices are in a match at a time.

Can I play on my own?

Most games include a practice mode against a bot, which needs only one phone and no connection at all. Chess offers easy, normal and hard computer opponents.

Which phones and browsers are supported?

Any phone released in roughly the last six years with an up-to-date browser will work. That covers Safari on iOS, Chrome on Android, and Chromium-based browsers such as Edge, Brave and Samsung Internet. The only hard requirement is WebRTC data channel support.

Is Fun Hub free?

Yes. All five games are free with no purchases, no subscription and no advertising.

What happens if the connection drops mid-game?

The game pauses and both phones show a reconnect prompt. Because pairing is tied to the room the host opened, rescanning the same QR code or reopening the join link puts you back in the same match.
